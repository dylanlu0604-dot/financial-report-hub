Still at the early innings of a multi-year upcycle
Tailwinds for substrates to strengthen further
We lift estimates for all three substrate makers to factor in accelerated growth from
capacity expansion and stronger than expected pricing amid industry undersupply
dynamic with meaningful uplift in margins; and raise PTs to factor in more margin upside
and earnings power expansion.  Substrates stocks have rallied on accelerated AI demand

that are driving pricing and margins to move up, but has taken a breather post Q1 results
and likely factored in concerns on oversupply from capacity expansions. We note that
most of the major substrate companies are looking to expand capacity by only 20-50%
in the coming years, suggesting further upside risk to pricing has yet to be priced in. We
believe there's still more upside to come  in a structurally different cycle driven by AI
which we anticipate much more favourable pricing among imbalance supply/demand

and valuation should drift higher as margins/earnings start to manifest. We lift our PTs
for Unimicron/Kinsus/NYPCB by 94%/70%/52% on earnings upward revisions and
moving from a P/BV-based approach to a P/E-based methodology, reflecting improving
earnings visibility and higher ROE that are expected to be stronger for longer.
Unimicron (Buy, lift PT to NT$1200): We continue to  like Unimicron as the clear
technology leader as key suppliers across NVDA GPU and major ASIC platforms. We now

forecast '26/'27/'28 sales to grow 37%/38%/25% YoY (up 4%/13%/13% from prior
sales estimates) with momentum to accelerate from H226E as a number of AI GPU/ASIC
project start to ramp with potential upside from AI CPUs and general server. We
anticipate high UTR and pricing uplifts will lead to 30-35% GM for ABF in H226 and
further improve to 35-40% level in 2027E. This will lead to 2026/2027/2028E GM of
21%/29%/32%, up from our prior forecasts of 20%/25%/26%. HDI margins should

gradually tick up on improving yield rates; while BT margins likely remain below
corporate average as the company adjust its production lines.

Q: Could ABF drive the next phase of growth for Unimicron?
Yes. Among Taiwanese suppliers, we believe Unimicron is best positioned to capitalise on rapid AI
deployment based on its comprehensive offerings across GPU, CPU and ASIC AI servers. We expect
Unimicron to gain share in Rubin GPUs, and will continue to benefit from growing AI server TAM as a

key supplier for several US cloud service providers' AI ASIC substrates. We flag other drivers including
size increases and content gains for general servers and switch when migrating to future generations.
We are positive on the outlook and see higher utilization rate, more substrate pricing uplifts,
improving yield rate could drive margin expansion in 2026E/2027E/2028E.
Q: Will the non-ABF substrate business grow?
Moderately with upside potential from HDI ramp, in our view. The company has not aggressively

expanded its RPCB (Rigid PCB) capacity, but chose to optimise HDI production as it shifts focus from
smartphones to AI servers. We believe Unimicron was the dominant high-density interconnect (HDI)
supplier of Hopper OAM boards although it has lost some share on the Blackwell to Victory Giant.
That being said, we are starting to see yield rate improvement for its Taiwan HDI fab, and anticipate
volume for this fab would start to ramp from Q226, driving more growth in 2026E/2027E.

We maintain a Buy rating as we continue to expect Unimicron to deliver above-industry sales growth
in 2025-29E, as we view it well positioned for  structural tailwinds from AI servers, general servers and
CPO deployment. We believe it is set for strong growth as a major substrate supplier for Nvidia GPU
and multiple ASIC substrates, its HDI business is finally gaining momentum, and  also it is involved in a
number of US CSP ASIC projects. We model sales to grow 37%/38%/25% in 2026/2027/2028E, led

by ABF substrates.
A number of  positive drivers for ABF substrate growth including: 1) AI servers: substantial volume
growth and content gains in new platforms , eg  at least 50-100% increase in area for ABF substrate
consumption from Hopper to Blackwell, and up another 50-100% from Blackwell to Rubin; 2)
general servers: body size increase and higher layer count to drive 20-50% content gains when
migrating to new platforms; 3) networking/switch1.6T/800G substrate capacity consumption

doubling from the 800G/400G migrations.
Unimicron is trading at 30x/19x  2027/2028E P/E, compared to Taiwan hardware/components peers
trading average at 33x/26x.  We believe it  has priced in the substrate price increases to date driven by
mostly material price hikes and some pricing premium; yet considering the industry is entering a
structurally different upcycle from AI server fundamentals with more margin upside  on the way, we
believe the stock has not fully factored in  the stronger pricing bargaining power supported by AI-

driven demand upside and stronger-than-expected margin expansion.

Yes, but it is improving given the strong memory pull-ins on top of tightening material supply has
been driving up BT substrate pricing. Just like for Taiwanese peers, we are not optimistic about

Kinsus' BT substrate business growth, as these applications' end-demand growth (including for
handsets, memory, automotive, etc) is likely to remain relatively mild. Tightening material supply
prioritizing high-end ABF substrates however is triggering some improvement in pricing which is
supplementing slow unit growth to also improve margins.

We also note that the company enjoys stronger pricing power given more orders based on spot prices

Q: Could BT substrate/PCB business remain a drag?
We are seeing some improvement as material supply constraints and rush orders have lifted pricing to
return these businesses toward profitability. We still are not as optimistic about  its BT substrate and
PCB business growth as the end-demand growth of these applications (handset, automotive, etc)

remains relatively muted despite the strength we've seen in memory.
We believe NYPCB is set up to enjoy strong sales growth from stronger pricing, 800G/1.6TB transition
while also gaining traction in ASIC projects; and should enjoy better pricing on strong sourcing
capabilities from its mother/sister group with better selling price given more orders based on spot
prices.  We revise up our 2026E/2027E/2028E earnings  by 23%/35%/85% to NT$12.72/NT$24.37/

NT$34.91 and lift our PT from NT$575 to NT$875, based on 25.1x 2028E P/E. That being said, we
maintain a Neutral rating as we keep balances on full valuation and anticipate growth to be capped
as its capacity expansion lags peers.
Based on our industry analysis, we forecast a number of ABF substrate upgrades, including: 1) 800G
switches consume an additional 100-120% capacity per unit compared to 400G; 2) 1.6T capacity
consumption is almost double versus 800G; 3) substantial substrate size increases and content gains

for both AI  and general servers when migrating to new platforms. On the flip side, we expect limited
content increases for BT substrates and PCBs as: 1) volume lacks visibility—they are subject to more
consumer electronics-centric demand with short product lives; 2) there are no clear signs of size or
layer count increases for new products.
NYPCB is trading at 26x/17x  2027/2028E P/E, compared to Taiwan hardware/components peers
trading average at 33x/26x. Expectations can swing sharply for the company with the business cycle.

The stock has rallied, pricing in stronger growth and margins upside risks from AI and Switch projects

now trading below industry peers average factoring in downside risk from business correction as its

Thoughts on technologies under development
1) Glass substrates
Compared to organic substrates, glass materials offer superior dimensional stability,
thermal conductivity, and electrical properties. For starters, glass core substrates
leverage TGV (Through Glass Via) technology for via formation that allows smaller via

sizes and higher density interconnects relative to the mechanical drilling used for ABF
substrates. In addition, traditional ABF substrates require additional warpage treatment
during layer build-up processing where glass substrate's flat surface allows more precise
lithography and multi-layer alignment.
Glass core substrates offer better performance and scalability for next-generation
chipsets and packaging requirements that have been driven by applications and

workloads requiring larger form factor packages, including high performance
computing, data centers, AI, graphics, higher speed capabilities, etc. The core layer of
ABF substrates, ie CCL, currently is dominated by Japanese supplier Resonac, which is
actively researching other materials in case of potentially being replaced by glass
manufacturers such as Corning, Schott, etc; while substrate makers are also exploring
potential alternatives other than glass.

2) CoWoP (Chip-On-Wafer-on-PCB)
CoWoP (Chip-on-Wafer-on-PCB) is an NVIDIA-led advanced packaging architecture,
developed with partners such as TSMC, that targets lower cost and improved scalability
versus CoWoS by eliminating the ABF substrate layer and directly bonding the silicon

interposer plus GPU/HBM assembly onto a large platform PCB. This substrate-less design
leverages mature PCB manufacturing to support high-speed NVLink interconnects and
HBM integration while enabling larger form factors for AI systems. Technically, CoWoP
retains a silicon interposer with TSVs and fine-pitch RDL for high-density interconnects,
with chip integration via micro-bump or hybrid bonding, but shifts system-level routing
to advanced PCB technologies such as HDI and mSAP (typically ~20–35 μm line/space).

This enables shorter interconnect paths, improving signal and power integrity, while the
lidless design supports more efficient thermal dissipation.
The primary advantage of CoWoP lies in cost and scalability. By removing the ABF
substrate and simplifying the packaging stack, it can deliver an estimated 30–50%
reduction in packaging cost while enabling panel-level manufacturing (e.g., ~450 × 450
mm), which improves throughput and capacity ramp timelines. However, the trade-off

lies in lower routing precision versus CoWoS and increased manufacturing challenges,
including PCB warpage, CTE mismatch, and the need for substrate-like precision in PCB
fabrication. As such, CoWoP is expected to complement rather than replace CoWoS,
which remains the preferred solution for highest-performance AI GPUs.

localized high-density interconnects while maintaining a simpler and more cost-efficient

EMIB-T builds upon the proven technology of EMIB by incorporating TSVs to enhance

Valuation Method and Risk Statement
Investing in the technology sector involves a high degree of risk. Rapid technological changes,
increasing competition and exposure to macroeconomic cycles are among the many risks
faced by investors in tech stocks. Moreover, it is extremely difficult to project the financial
results of tech companies since their operating models are highly volatile and unpredictable.
Finally, valuing tech stocks can prove challenging as neither traditional nor non-traditional

valuation measures provide much insight into how these stocks trade.
Our PT for Unimicron is based on P/E multiple methodology.
Downside risks include weaker AI server/ABF substrate demand, utilization and pricing
pressure from capacity expansion, limited margin upside from long-term contracts, input cost
volatility (ABF, T-glass, CCL), execution risks in overseas expansion, rising competition, and
packaging technology transitions.
Our PT for Kinsus is based on P/E multiple methodology.

Downside risks include lower-than-expected share gains in AI server projects, lower-than-
expected global PC/server demand, oversupply from capacity expansion, raw material cost
increases, intensified competition and a lack of spec upgrades and content increases.
Our price target for Nan Ya Printed Circuit Board is based on P/E multiple methodology.
Upside risks include faster-than-expected ramp-up of networking/switches, stronger-than-
expected pricing increase driving margins back to 2021-22 profitability level, better-than-

expected consumer demand, and continued tightness in BT substrates.
Downside risks include lower-than-expected ramp-up of networking/switch projects, lower-
than-expected global PC/server demand, oversupply from capacity expansions, raw material
cost increases, intensified competition, and a lack of spec upgrades and content increase.
As for industry risks, substrate applications are dominated by PCs, but servers, artificial
intelligence and advanced computing are driving demand growth. We believe key risks to the

industry include slower-than-expected growth in servers, networking, and high performance
computing, ASP pressure, margin erosion, and lack of spec upgrades. We believe the flexible
circuit board and high-density interconnect board segments are more vulnerable to
fluctuations in the consumer product cycle and have lower visibility.

Key takeaways from the Deutsche Bank Global Auto, Mobility & Robotics 
Conference 
Held at the Deutsche Bank Center in New York, the recent Global Autos, Mobility 
& Robotics Conference brought together industry leaders for two days of 
discussions on innovation, strategy, and the next generation of solutions and 
capabilities. The event provided a comprehensive view of how the sector is 
evolving – highlighting both near-term resilience and the structural shifts 
reshaping the industry. From changing demand dynamics and geopolitical 
pressures to the rise of new technologies and growth areas, the conversations 
underscore a sector in transition. These key conference themes capture the core 
insights shaping the outlook for autos, mobility, and robotics today. 
Resilience for now, but risks building 
Auto markets in Europe and the US remain relatively resilient, with demand 
holding up better than expected in the near term. However, visibility is limited 
and companies are increasingly cautious about the outlook. Geopolitical 
tensions – particularly in the Middle East – are a key risk, with the potential to 
push up costs and weigh on demand. While impacts so far have been contained, 
a more challenging environment is expected if pressures persist into 2027. 
Costs rising, pricing power limited 
Cost pressures are building across energy, raw materials, and logistics, and are 
starting to flow through supply chains. While companies have partly offset this 
through hedging and cost controls, pricing power remains limited – especially in 
the US and Europe. Rather than raising prices directly, firms are relying on 
product mix and model upgrades, creating ongoing tension between protecting 
margins and sustaining demand. 
Geopolitics driving cost pressures 
Direct exposure to the Middle East remains limited for most companies, but the 
conflict is beginning to impact costs – particularly in energy and logistics. While 
hedging has softened the near-term effect, pressures are expected to build into 
2027 as protections roll off. Early signs of supply chain inflation are emerging, 
with higher costs gradually passed through the system, often with a lag, and 
companies stepping up efficiency measures to offset the impact on margins. 
New growth areas emerging beyond autos 
Suppliers are increasingly looking beyond automotive markets to drive future 
growth, targeting areas such as data centres, energy systems, and defence. 
While these segments remain small today, they are seen as important medium-
term opportunities. Continued investment and partnerships are expected to 
gradually scale these businesses and diversify revenue streams over time.

調査本部 主席研究員 齋藤 尚登

[要約]
