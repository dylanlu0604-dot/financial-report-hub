HD현대중공업은 전일(9/10) 대출력 발전엔진 (20MW, 9MW 급) 및 SMR 캐파 
확장을 위한 신규시설투자를 공시했다. 규모는 총 1조 722억원이며, 그 중 엔진 관련 
투자는 8,336억원, SMR 관련 투자는 2,386억원이다.

엔진 관련 투자에서는 4행정 중속엔진 캐파 확장을 위한 신규 공장 신설 및 기존 
공장 생산 설비 구축을 진행하고자 한다. 동사의 4행정 중속엔진 캐파는 기존 3GW 
규모이며, 이번 증설을 통해 신규 공장 캐파 3GW, 기존 공장 증설분 1.2GW가 
더해져 2028년 5월 기준 최종적으로 7.2GW 규모가 될 것으로 예상된다. 영암 
공장과 신축 공장에서는 데이터센터향으로 납품될 9MW 및 20MW 규모의 대출력 
엔진 위주, 기존 울산 공장은 3~5MW 규모의 선박용 엔진 위주의 생산이 이루어질 
계획이다. 이번 공장 신축 완료 이후에도 가용 부지가 남아있어 시장 수요에 따라 
향후 추가적인 캐파 확대 역시 고려할 수 있다.

SMR 투자의 경우 현대건설/테라파워와의 협력을 위한 SMR 주기기 연 2기 생산 
캐파 확보를 목적으로 진행되었다. 1기 당 출력 용량은 345MW이며, 구체적인 생산

I. The Silver Tsunami
The “silver tsunami” refers to the large wave of Baby Boomers moving into retirement and the rise in the number
of older households. That demographic shift is increasingly reshaping America’s consumer economy, as older
households account for a growing share of income and spending power.
The scale of that shift becomes clearer when viewed against the broader expansion of the household sector. The
US had 137.1 million households in 2025, more than twice as many as in the late 1960s (chart). That expanding
household base has created a much larger pool of consumers earning and spending income.

### Page 2

Just as important is the change in the age composition of those households. The number headed by someone 65 or
older has climbed to 41.9 million, far above any other age group (chart). Most younger cohorts have grown much
more slowly. That's because by 2029, everyone in the large Baby Boomer cohort will be 65 years old or older, and
they are living longer.
As a result, the 65+ group now accounts for 30.6% of all US households, up from roughly one-fifth around the

turn of the century (chart). Nearly one in three households is now headed by a senior.

### Page 3

Over time, the 65+ cohort has taken up a steadily larger share of the household sector, while the shares
represented by several younger age groups have declined (chart).
Income follows a clear life-cycle pattern. Household income generally rises through the working years, peaks in
middle age, and then falls after retirement. Mean income was $163,600 for households headed by 45- to 54-year-
olds in 2025, compared with $92,400 for the 65+ group (chart).

### Page 4

Median income tells the same basic story. It is highest among households headed by 45- to 54-year-olds and
lowest among households headed by seniors, confirming that the pattern is not simply being driven by a small
number of high-income households distorting the mean (chart).
But here is where the silver tsunami starts to matter for aggregate spending power. Seniors earn less per
household, but there are a lot more of them. Multiplying mean income by the number of households shows that

the 65+ group collectively received $3.87 trillion in money income in 2025, more than any other age cohort
(chart).

### Page 5

That has pushed the senior share of household income sharply higher. Households headed by someone 65 or older
accounted for 22.3% of aggregate household income in 2025, roughly double their share in the late 1960s and
now the largest of any age group (chart).
The overall household income pool has also become enormous. Total money income reached a record $17.37
trillion in 2025, up substantially over the past decade (chart). This provides a large base of purchasing power to

support consumer spending.

### Page 6

But that income is not evenly distributed. The highest-income fifth of households received 52.4% of aggregate
income in 2025, while the top 5% alone received 23.5% (chart). The lowest two fifths together received just over
one-tenth.
Mean income was $331,800 for the highest quintile and $594,500 for the top 5%, compared with just $19,100 for
the lowest quintile (chart).

### Page 7

Taken together, the story is straightforward. America has more households, those households are increasingly
older, and a growing share of income is attributable to older and higher-income consumers. That helps explain
why aggregate spending remains resilient even as younger and lower-income households face more financial
pressure. It also fits our “G-shaped” economy thesis: Consumer strength is increasingly supported by generational
wealth, not current income alone.

Baby Boomers have accumulated enormous wealth over their working lives and can continue to spend out of
those balance sheets in retirement. Indeed, household net worth reached $185.6 trillion in Q2-2026, with Baby
Boomers alone holding $97.4 trillion (chart). That wealth is a key reason the silver tsunami remains a key driver
of consumer spending.
II. The Golden Economy
The latest economic data suggest that the US economy remains on a solid growth path. Consumer spending

remains strong, hiring is picking up, manufacturing activity is improving, and expected Q3 GDP growth is
running at a robust pace. Let's take a closer look:
(1) Consumer spending. Redbook same-store retail sales rose 8.2% y/y during the week ended September 18,
remaining well above the pace seen through much of the past several years (chart).

### Page 8

(2) Hiring activity. The latest ADP weekly data suggest that labor demand remains strong. Private employers
added an average of 20,000 jobs per week during the four weeks through September 5, the most since June 20
and the third consecutive weekly increase (chart).
(3) Manufacturing activity. The average of the New York and Philadelphia Fed manufacturing indexes remained
elevated at 22.7 in September, suggesting that manufacturing activity continued to expand during the month

(chart).

### Page 9

(4) GDP. The Atlanta Fed’s GDPNow model currently estimates 5.1% real GDP growth (saas) in Q3 (chart). Real
final sales to private domestic purchasers are expected to rise 4.7%, the strongest since Q2-2021. Consumer
spending is expected to increase 4.1%, the strongest since Q1-2023, while business investment is on track for its
strongest three-quarter stretch since 2021.

Within R&D  spend, we analyse the largest cost component –  model training

 Effective GPU capacity, which depends on the GPU's peak performance and

 Cost per GPU hour , which is affected by utility costs and hardware efficiency

Reduced training volume (in terms of effective token-interaction workload)
through sparse-attention architectures.  In Transformer models, attention
determines how each token incorporates information from other tokens in the
sequence. Full attention requires every token to interact with every other token, making

compute scale quadratically with context length. Sparse attention reduces this burden
by limiting attention to a subset of relevant tokens, thereby lowering effective token-to-
token interactions in long-context training and inference. Given more constrained
compute-resource availability, our channel checks of industry experts indicate China's AI
labs are putting more effort into sparse-attention designs to reduce compute
consumption. In comparison, US peers may place greater emphasis on model quality

intensive design choice. For instance, DeepSeek V3.2 uses DeepSeek Sparse Attention
(DSA) to reduce the workload, while DeepSeek V4 adds context compression before the
sparse design based on V3.2 to improve long-context efficiency.

2. Increasing GPU throughput by adopting lower compute precision;

multiple approaches to improve MFU
As discussed in our training cost framework, in addition to reducing total compute
workload on the numerator side, AI labs can also lower total training costs by enlarging
the denominator, that is by increasing the effective computing capacity per GPU hour.
We see China's labs placing greater emphasis on lower compute precision.
Under the same GPU, lower precision can significantly increase throughput, as fewer
bits are used per value, enabling more calculations per unit time while reducing memory

usage and data movement. Taking the Nvidia B200 as an example, moving from BF16/
FP16 to FP8 can roughly double theoretical GPU throughput. While large-scale model
training has historically relied on BF16/FP16 mixed precision, we see China's AI labs
pushing the frontier of lower-precision training. Examples include DeepSeek-V3's FP8
mixed-precision training framework and DeepSeek V4's FP4 quantisation-aware training
for select model components. Meanwhile, Baidu's ERNIE 4.5 and 5.0 adopt FP8 mixed-

precision training, while Alibaba's Qwen3.5 introduces a native FP8 pipeline and FP8
end-to-end training techniques.

infrastructure teams to optimise compute resource utilisation. For instance, with its end-

of computational efficiency for model inference activities, higher than the industry

average of 40-50%. Specifically, MiniMax has implemented:
 Unified training and inference computing , with an intelligent scheduling
system that dynamically allocates computing resources between training and
inference tasks, maximising cluster utilisation without compromising
performance.
 Cross-cluster load balancing , which enables flexible resource distribution,
lowers latency and enhances reliability and disaster recovery.

1. Model-side optimisation: Lower compute and memory usage
Under constrained compute resources, China's model labs continue to innovate in
model architecture to lower inference costs. Compared with training, inference provides

additional optimisation levers, allowing labs to reduce overall model scale, lower active
compute (through Mixture of Experts, or MoE), adopt sparse attention and compress
memory usage (namely KV-cache compression).
 Mixture of Experts reduces active parameters. China's frontier models tend to
have smaller total parameter scales than US counterparts, which lowers their
baseline compute requirements. At the inference stage, MoE provides an
additional layer of sparsity by routing each token to only a subset of expert

networks, rather than activating the full model for every token. While we
acknowledge that MoE is not unique to China and was initially developed by
overseas labs, China's model developers appear more proactive in deploying it at
scale, improving model sparsity (by lowering the active-parameter ratio) while still
enhancing model performance. Given limited visibility into closed-source overseas
models, we note that earlier overseas MoE models such as GPT-4, Grok-1 and

Llama 4 Scout typically activated about 15-30% of total parameters, while China's
leading MoE models usually activate only about 3-10%. We view DeepSeek as a
notable example: its MoE active-parameter ratio declined from about 5% in
DeepSeek V3.2 to about 3% in V4 Pro, while more efficient parameter activation
still supported further improvements in model intelligence (AA Intelligence Index
rising from 42 to 52).
 Sparse attention reduces long-context compute.  Similar to the training

phase, attention design is another inference-efficiency lever. Moving from full
attention to sparse attention can reduce the number of token interactions,
especially in long-context workloads such as AI coding, agents and long-
document tasks, lowering effective compute required per request. At the
inference stage, the sparse attention architecture can be combined with KV-cache
compression, further lowering compute workloads.
 While memory-related costs dominate inference costs, KV-cache

compression can significantly reduce them.  Based on an academic  paper,
cached input is the largest cost component in inference, accounting for about

2. Serving and engineering optimisation: Higher throughput from
better workload orchestration
Beyond model architecture, AI developers can further reduce inference costs by
optimising the AI infrastructure layer (namely compute clusters), improving chip
utilisation and serving throughput. Compared with training, where optimisation is

mainly focused on keeping large-scale clusters stable and improving MFU, inference
serving has more heterogeneous workloads and therefore more room for workload
separation, batching and real-time scheduling.
 P/D separation improves resource matching.  We view P/D separation as an
inference-specific lever. While training workloads are relatively more uniform, LLM
inference consists of two distinct phases: prefill (processing the input prompt and

building KV cache) and decode (generating output tokens). These two phases
have different resource profiles: prefill is more compute-heavy, while decode is
more memory- and bandwidth-heavy, as well as latency-sensitive. Prefill and
decode disaggregation separates the two workloads into different GPU pools,
allowing each phase to use more suitable resources and reducing interference
between long prompts and token generation.
 Batching improves GPU utilisation.  We view batching as more important in

inference than training. Training naturally processes large batches of data, while
inference demand arrives in fragmented, real-time user requests with different
prompt lengths and output lengths. Batching combines multiple inference
requests so GPUs process more tokens together rather than serving each request
independently. Continuous and in-flight batching further improves throughput by
admitting new requests as soon as finished sequences leave the batch, rather than

waiting for the slowest request to finish. This helps raise GPU utilisation and
reduces cost per token.
 Scheduling reduces idle time and load imbalance.  Similar to the training
phase, inference systems need to keep expensive GPUs working rather than
waiting. Better scheduling (e.g., unified training and inference compute, cross-
load balancing, routing between different model sizes and clusters) can reduce idle
time, smooth traffic spikes, and improve overall cluster utilisation.

Framing potential upside in China AI's global share
To gauge the future market structure and potential upside in the global market share for
China's AI model companies, we use other 'China-going-global' industries as analogies
under three scenarios:
In a cost-led scenario, we believe China's model companies could gain
meaningful global share, underpinned by their structural cost advantages.  If
China's models eventually converge with global leaders in capability and model usage

becomes largely commoditised (i.e., users see limited difference when calling different
models), we believe the closest analogy could be solar photovoltaic (PV). The solar PV
market is largely driven by scale, cost and supply-chain competition, where China has
built a dominant global position, with an 80%-plus global share and up to about 95%
share in some key supply-chain stages. Under this scenario, China's model companies
could leverage structural inference-cost advantages to capture a large share of global

token demand.
In a cost-performance scenario, China's model companies should have room to
gain share outside the most premium or frontier use cases.  China's models may
still lag global leaders in absolute intelligence but maintain a clear pricing advantage. We
believe this could be comparable to EVs, smartphones and home appliances, where
China's companies have gained about 30-50% share in select global categories and
markets by offering "good enough and lower-cost" products, supported by product

iteration, scale, channel expansion and improving brand recognition. Under this
scenario, we expect China's model companies to gain meaningful share in mid-range
and price-sensitive AI workloads, especially in high-volume use cases where frontier-
model performance is not essential.
In a performance- or ecosystem-led scenario, China's global share upside may
be capped if the intelligence gap remains meaningful.  If US frontier models
maintain a clear capability lead and AI  becomes embedded into enterprise workflows

(via AI agents), competition may shift from standalone model pricing to  ecosystem build-
out. We observe that global AI leaders are proactively moving in this direction: Anthropic
is expanding  from Claude Code into broader workflow products such as Claude
Cowork, Skills, MCP and enterprise plugins, while OpenAI is building Codex into a
developer and enterprise agent platform through Apps SDK, MCP integrations and
Codex plugins, according to the company. In this scenario, the user data and workflow

accumulations may become potential barriers to share shift, even if China's models offer
lower prices. We think the analogy could be closer to cloud or operating system, where
the global share of China's companies  remains in the low single digits to below 10%.
Under this scenario, China's model companies may still expand in select price-sensitive,
open-source or sovereign-AI markets, but their overall global revenue share may remain
relatively low. That said, China's AI labs are also actively building their own ecosystems,

with examples including Moonshot AI's Kimi Work integrating multiple productivity
applications, and Tencent opening WeChat's AI ecosystem capabilities to developers.

A potentially tiered global AI model market ahead
We see the cost-performance scenario as the more likely base case, as the global
model market is likely to become increasingly tiered, considering:
 B2B and B2C use cases have different capability requirements.  In B2B
scenarios, especially AI agents (e.g., AI coding), models need to handle more
complex workflows, tool use, long-context reasoning and error recovery. As these

use cases are directly linked to productivity improvement, we believe enterprise
users should be more willing to pay for stronger model capability. In contrast,
many B2C chatbot use cases are less complex, with users mainly requiring simple
Q&A and conversational interaction, making them less sensitive to incremental
frontier-model improvements, in our view.
 Agentic workloads are likely to be tiered by task complexity. Even within AI
agents, higher-end models may still be required for more difficult tasks (such as

project-level planning, architecture design and complex debugging), where
reasoning depth, planning capability and tool-use reliability matter more.
However, repetitive execution tasks (including routine code generation, document
processing, customer support and standardised enterprise operations) could be
served by lower-cost models once workflows become sufficiently structured, in our
view.
 Compute constraints could make frontier capacity a strategic scarce

resource. We believe this could further reinforce model tiering, as frontier AI
capability is not only driven by model algorithms, but also by a system-level
engineering stack across high-end GPUs and HBM, interconnect, data-centre
power availability and serving efficiency. With agent adoption driving much higher
inference intensity, customers may allocate premium models only to high-value
tasks where the incremental productivity gain justifies the cost, while routing

simpler and high-volume workloads to cheaper models running on lower-cost
hardware. In our view, this supports a multi-model market structure, with frontier
models priced as scarce intelligence capacity and cost-performance models serving
broader demand.
 High-end intelligence may not be suitable for unrestricted use. As models

WHAT'S PRICED IN?
China's AI foundation model companies have delivered strong share
performances since their listings in January, despite some recent moderation. For
instance,  MiniMax have risen 214% as of 15 June 2026.  We think this was mainly driven
by rising revenue growth expectations (e.g.,  MiniMax's management set internal targets
of US$1bn ARR by end-2026, compared to ~US150m in Feb 2026), as investors see

rising token demand amid the rapid scaling of AI agent products across OpenClaw and
AI coding agents. Meanwhile, continued model iteration and capital-market updates
(e.g., HSCI and HSTECH inclusion; proposed A-share listings) are additional boosts to
share performance, in our view.
While investor attention on AI foundation model companies remains high, we think
investors also recognise that China model stocks' current valuations are at
elevated levels versus global peers (e.g., OpenAI and Anthropic are valued at below

30x P/ARR in the private market). Investors are likely to continue monitoring potential
catalysts over the next few months, including  model upgrades, IPO lock-up expiry, Stock
Connect inclusion,  progress on A-share listings as well as global AI leaders'  listing in US
market.
That said, we believe the market still underestimates China model companies'
cost-performance advantages and their long-term upside potential from global
share gains. We expect global investors to increasingly recognise China AI models'

cost-efficiency advantage, especially as the market shifts from pursuing token-usage
quantity (i.e., “tokenmaxxing”) towards token-usage quality, with more emphasis on
ROI. Meanwhile, as the capability gap with US models continues to narrow and China's
models maintain structural inference-pricing advantages, we believe they could
accelerate global AI penetration and support China AI models' global share gains.
Despite the premium valuations, we anticipate significant, long-term growth upside

ahead for China's leading  model companies, which may help digest China AI model
companies'  (e.g., MiniMax)  valuation multiples as revenue scaling crystallises.

### Page 2

Two shocks hit the stock market last week. On Monday, the heads of the major AI labs called for slowing the
development of frontier models, and chip stocks sold off hard. On Wednesday, the FOMC raised the federal funds
rate by 25bps to 3.75%-4.00%, the first hike since July 2023, and the updated Dot Plot points to one more this
year. The S&P 500 fell 0.1%, with eight of the 11 sectors declining.
The Health Care sector (OW) rose 1.8%, and Communication Services (MW) was close behind at 1.2%. Utilities

(OW) fell the most, down 3.0%, while Financials (OW) and Real Estate (UW) both fell 2.3%.
Let's take a closer look at Financials, Communication Services, and Industrials:
(1) Financials. Financials was the second-worst performer this week, but the Fed wasn't the main reason. On
Monday, Bank of America CEO Brian Moynihan told the Barclays Global Financial Services Conference that Q3
investment banking fees will come in between $1.6 billion and $1.8 billion, down from $2.0 billion a year earlier.

Moynihan cited Dealogic data showing investment banking fees across the market down about 10%. The IPO
window has not helped, with OpenAI ruling out a 2026 listing. Bank of America fell 5.1%, and the group fell
with it. Investment Banking & Brokerage declined 4.4% this week, the second-worst industry in the sector, while
Regional Banks fell 4.8% and Diversified Banks fell 4.0% (chart).
Analysts also expect the deal boom to fade. They just put it a year out. Their consensus estimates represent

earnings growth for Investment Banking & Brokerage companies of 30.7% this year but just 9.7% in 2027

(chart). Moynihan says the fade is happening now.
None of that has dented the fundamentals. Forward earnings is up 12.5% ytd, but the price is up just 1.8%
because the forward P/E is down 9.5% (chart).
The Financials sector’s companies collectively trade at 14.7 times forward earnings versus 18.9 for the S&P 500,
a discount the sector has carried since 2010 (chart). The sector's forward profit margin is at a record 22.1%. We
are retaining our overweight rating on Financials.

### Page 4

(2) Communication Services. Within Communication Services, up 1.2% this week, Interactive Media Services
did the work, rising 2.9% (chart).
Monday's selloff ran on fears that a slower AI LLM training race means less demand for compute, as the SOXX
gave back 5.5%. Nevertheless, there is a large backlog in the demand for compute as measured by remaining
performance obligations of the hyperscalers (chart).

### Page 5

Alphabet and Meta account for 78.4% of the sector's market capitalization and 70.0% of its forward earnings.
Interactive Media has a 29.4% forward profit margin versus 21.7% for Communication Services as a whole, and
analysts expect earnings to grow 73.0% this year versus 55.9% for the sector (chart).
The sector trades at a forward P/E of 18.4 even though analysts collectively expect an earnings decline in 2027
of 11.3% (chart). Google and Meta represent 70% of the sector’s earnings and the entire growth story. Remove

them, and the fundamentals do not support the multiple. We are retaining our market-weight rating on
Communication Services.

### Page 6

(3) Industrials. Industrials is down 3.1% mtd and still up 9.2% ytd. Last week's selloff hit its best performers.
Construction Machinery & Heavy Trucks and Rail Transportation lead the sector ytd at 29.7% and 20.2%,
and last week they were the sector's two worst industries, down 2.1% and 2.4% (chart).
Construction Machinery's 2026 earnings forecast has doubled to 40.1% from 18% in January. A pause in
frontier AI model training does not cancel orders already placed. Rail revenue growth for 2026 has jumped to

8.8% from 2.9% in March.
We flagged the sector’s valuation risk in April, and it has resolved in our favor. The sector's forward P/E was
25.5 back then versus 20.9 for the broader S&P 500 index. It is now 22.4 against 18.9 (chart). Electrical
Components has been devalued, with its forward P/E dropping from 28.7 to 24.4, even as its forward profit
margin has risen to 18.2%.

### Page 7

Industrials still trades at a premium to the S&P 500 multiple on slower earnings growth expectations. The
analysts’ consensus for long-term earnings growth is 18.0% compared with 26.6% for the S&P 500. We are
retaining our overweight rating on Industrials.

2. エネルギー危機と政治不安の中で経済の体質改善は遅れたまま

引き上げに慎重である。一方、2月に生じたイラン発のエネルギーショックに伴いインフレが強含みであるため、実
質金利は依然としてマイナス圏にある。加えて、長期化する政治不安のため政府による財政引き締めが一向に進
まず、名目 GDP（国内総生産）の 8%程度の財政赤字が常態化している。

ボロジャン首相が率いる連立政権が崩壊した後も、同首相は暫定政権を率いている。この間、ニクショル・ダン

3. 政治不安と改革後退がさらなる通貨暴落を招く恐れ 
ルーマニアレウの対ユーロ実質レートは、コロナショック前後から名目レートとの乖離が顕著となっており、10 年
前に比べると 10%程度、増価している（図表 5）。一方、名目レートは 15%ほど減価しているため、この間の実質
レートの増価の主因は高インフレとなる。この高インフレは、ルーマニア経済のファンダメンタルズの悪化に伴うも
のであるから、ルーマニアレウの価値は過大に評価されており、修正＝下落余地が大きいと考えられる。

したがって、何らかのリスク要因が強く意識されると、ルーマニアの通貨は暴落を余儀なくされる公算が大きい。
足元では、政治不安の高まりが最大のリスク要因となる。依然として組閣ができないルーマニアであるが、この状
態が長期化すれば、ルーマニアが EU との間で約束している構造改革が後退し、EU からの支援が縮小ないし
は撤回される可能性が意識される。

現在、ダン大統領のもとで、ルーマニアは EU との間で約束している 6 つの主要改革法の公布を急いでいる。
6 つのうち、すでに 4 つの法律（国土計画・都市計画・建設に関する法、冷暖房部門の脱炭素化に関する法、国
税庁（ANAF）・税関職員の給与改革法、公務員キャリア制度改革法）は公布済みだが、残り 2 法（公職者の資産
申告や利益相反に関する規則を見直す法、公務員給与法）に関しては公務員の反発が強い。

こうした構造改革は、EU 復興基金からの資金配分を受けるための必須条件（コンディショナリティー）となって
いる。法が全て公布されても、政治不安の長期化で、ルーマニアの政権が構造改革の推進に向けた姿勢が堅持
できなくなると投資家が判断すれば、EU や ECB からのサポートが後退する可能性が意識され、レウ相場の下落
につながると懸念される。政治不安の早期収束と構造改革の推進がレウ相場の先行きを大きく左右する。
