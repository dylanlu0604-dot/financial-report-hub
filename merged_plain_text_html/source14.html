戰火影響料屬短期，無礙市場中期走勢
2026 年 4 月
美國對伊朗展開襲擊所引發的中東戰火延續至今，拖累 3 月截至 27 日的全球金融股市表現，
市場在多重變數交織下動能顯著受壓，主要股市指數單月報酬全盤皆墨。不過，在能源類股大漲的支
撐下，S&P 500 指數表現相對其他股市穩定。日本股市雖亦出現獲利了結壓力，但受惠於高市政府
「負責且積極的財政政策」 及日圓匯率弱勢有利出口 ， 今年以來 MSCI 日本指數的表現僅次於拉丁美
洲股市。另一方面，受油價高漲帶動通膨預期、外資持續撤出且經濟放緩所累，印度股市出現賣壓，

MSCI 印度指數不論在 3 月或是今年至今表現都是敬陪末座。
全球主要債市 3 月報酬亦全數下跌。在所有券種之中 ， 以新興當地債下跌 5.21% 表現最為疲弱 ，
除了油價引發市場對於進口原油的新興國家前景的擔憂外 ，高漲的美元指數也使得當地貨幣計價新興
債表現呈壓。 全球主權債同樣因為油價高漲推升通膨疑慮 ，投資人對於各國央行貨幣政策或因而轉為
更為鷹派高度警戒，使其表現差強人意。
自去年川普關稅「解放日」後美股跌至低點後開始走揚，各國股市與主要指數持續上揚，其中又
以金礦指數、南韓股市、費半指數、日本股市表現最佳。然而，自中東戰事 2/28 爆發後，以指數修
正幅度來看，跌幅最大的市場分別為金礦指數、南韓股市、日本股市。唯有費半指數則在 AI 需求暢
旺的加持下成為例外。由此可見，本波市場的回跌，基本上為「漲多跌多」的修正效應。
就基本面來看，2026 年美股企業獲利增長主軸仍在 AI， 由於2026 年仍在 AI 基礎建設期擴張間 ，
AI 終端需求仍是科技龍頭企業，故所帶動的獲利增長料不致受油價影響，今年單季盈餘增長可望維

持 2 位數水準。自中東戰事以來，彭博對於 MSCI 美國指數 Q1 及 Q2 的 EPS 預期不跌反漲，顯示市
場對於美國企業的獲利前景仍極具信心。同時，目前的資訊科技類股的預期 P/E 比與過去 10 年的均
值相較仍過有遭到低估之嫌，也預示科技類股相較於其他類股仍有進一步向上的動能。

預期變動影響較大，故在年期與券種的選擇上可以 7~15 年的中長期美國公債或投資級債為主。

美國股市：標普 500 企業盈餘增長加速，私募信貸贖回潮不致衝擊美國經濟
美國第四季 GDP 季增年率修正值 0.7%，較初值 1.4%有所調降，主要反映實質個人消費季比降
至 0.7%。第四季 GDP 數據整體仍支持美國經濟穩健。但第一季受通膨(關稅與伊朗危機)與高利率影
響，消費行為呈現「降級消費」特徵，非必需性支出收縮，經濟呈現倚賴「高收入族群支撐消費」的
K 型分化格局。由於中東戰火推高能源價格，美國民眾擔心生活成本壓力惡化，美國密西根大學3 月
消費者信心指數降至 55.5，較 2 月終值 56.6 下降 1.1，觸及今年以來最低紀錄，反映中東危機與生

活成本壓力削弱民眾樂觀情緒。
2 月非農就業人數淨減少 9.2 萬個，低於彭博預期的增加 1.3 萬，失業率躍升至 4.4%，失業率雖
短期上揚、但就長期角度仍在低檔。追究原因數據主要是受到罷工和天氣等短期因素干擾，導致醫療
健保、建築業、零售業和休閒酒店等產業表現不佳，但整體勞動市場狀態溫和，從請領就業金人數等
數據看來就業市場尚未出現顯著惡化跡象。市場先前擔憂 AI 取代人力，目前數據看來企業僅是招募
保守，後續可觀察勞動市場降溫是否反映在薪資成長，若薪資壓力下降有助於抑制服務業通膨，除直
接改善企業成本結構外，也將使聯準會擔憂的服務業通膨出現放緩。
預估第一季標普 500 企業餘年增仍可達到 11.3%，且在第二季顯著拉升至近兩成水準，科技板
塊受惠於 AI 仍是帶動美股企業獲利增長主軸。大型科技巨擘透過AI 優化流程，開始對利潤率產生正
面影響。然而，此一效益目前高度集中於資本與技術優勢明顯的科技龍頭企業，中小企業與非科技產
業尚未顯著受益。因此，企業獲利將呈現明顯分化格局，而尚未受惠於 AI 的非科技業者，將會在未

來幾年逐步提高使用 AI 程度，傳統企業大規模導入 AI 模型與使用算力，將可成為 AI 龍頭下一波業
績成長動能。
近期美國私募信貸市場爆發贖回潮，包含貝萊德、阿波羅、藍鷹(Blue Owl)等巨頭均遭投資人要

險上升，以及對高收益資產的獲利了結。研判此類事件雖造成金融股拉回，但引發總體系統性危機的
可能性極低。

歐洲股市：歐元區經濟表現穩健，央行面對能源危機採取警戒態度
歐元區第四季 GDP 季增率自初值的 0.3%下修至 0.2%，年增則下修至 1.2%，二者皆低於市場預
期。歐元區第四季經濟動能略微放緩，成長主要由內需帶動。家庭消費在穩健的勞動力市場與薪資增
長支撐下，季增率上升至 0.2%，固定資本形成雖由高點回落，但仍維持正成長，外部需求疲軟導致
出口季減，加上企業去庫存，顯示內需支撐正與貿易逆風強烈拉鋸。
三月份歐元區經濟活動連續 15 個月處於擴張區間，其中製造業表現強勁，歐元區製造業PMI 由
50.8 上升至 51.4，創 45 個月高點，受新訂單增長支撐，惟出口訂單連續第 49 個月低於榮枯線，外

需疲軟仍未改善。服務業受中東戰爭引發的不確定性與能源成本飆升衝擊，驟降至 50.1，放緩至 10
個月低點。預料南歐以觀光業為主國家將面對能源成本衝擊，而外銷為主的如德國、法國將迎來能源
與運輸成本飆升的雙重打擊。歐盟決定延後提交原定 4/15 提出將俄羅斯石油進口永久禁令入法的提
案，自 2022 年俄羅斯全面入侵烏克蘭以來，歐盟已大幅降低對俄能源依賴，是否因為伊朗戰爭調整
制裁內容將是未來關注重點。
歐洲央行在 3 月利率決策會議決定利率不變、但態度轉鷹。歐洲央行總裁拉加德表示，若通膨出
現「顯著但不過於持久」的超標情況，可能進行「適度的政策調整」，以確保通膨預期維持穩定。她

2 碼，顯示歐洲央行與英國央行面對能源危機採取警戒態度。

日本受惠於企業強勁支出 ，2025 年第四季 GDP 年化季增率為 1.3%， 優於原先預估的0.2%。2026
年不僅企業資本支出動能持續 ， 在高市早苗出任首相後 ， 政府選定支持包含實體人工智慧 、 再生醫學 、
量子運算與海洋無人機等 61 個項目，目標在 2040 年前取得全球 AI 機器人逾 30%的市占率，國產
晶片銷售額也要在同年衝上 40 兆日圓。高市希望藉由政府與民間的合作推動投資，強化發展包含人
工智慧、半導體、宇宙及航太等 17 大重點產業。由於自民黨已在國會選舉取得空前壓倒性勝利，產

業補貼計畫將可落實，讓政府投資不僅貢獻短期 GDP，亦可提升日本長期經濟競爭力。
日本 2 月剔除生鮮食品的消費者價格年比上漲 1.6%，創 2022 年 3 月以來最小升幅，主要因為
公用事業補貼壓低能源成本。日本勞動組合總連合會(連合)近期公布 2026 年春鬥第 1 次彙整結果，
加薪幅度雖低於去年同期 、 不過已連3 年高於 5%，且2026 年中小企業加薪幅度可望高於過去幾年，

Building activity softer than expected in the March quarter
Total building activity fell 3.5% over the quarter – below our forecast for a modest rise.
March’s drop was a result of a 4% fall in the amount of non-residential building activity. That continues
a down trend seen over the past two years.
Residential building was down 2%. That’s a modest drop, with the level of residential building
effectively remaining flat over the past year.

Residential activity
March saw a 2% drop in residential building activity. That sort of move is well within the normal range of
quarterly swings. 
Stepping back and looking at the longer-term trend, overall residential building activity has been
broadly flat over the past year. However, there are notable differences across the country - while
building activity in Auckland has continued to gradually lift, activity in other regions has eased. 
We expect to see home building activity turn higher through the latter part of the year, with the number
of new dwellings consented up 11% over the past year. 
However, what’s less clear is whether that momentum will be sustained as we head into 2027, with
interest rates and building costs pushing higher, while house prices remain soft. Combined with
broader uncertainty about the economic outlook, developers are likely to be cautious about initiating
new projects over the months ahead.

Non-residential activity
Non-residential building levels have continued to trend down, falling 4% over the past quarter. That is
the ninth consecutive quarter of decline, with softness in economic activity discouraging the
development of new commercial space. 
In our recent talks with businesses, a number of those we spoke to indicated that they’re holding off on
major capital expenditure for the time being due to uncertainty about the economic landscape.
Consistent with that, we expect some further softness over the remainder of this year.

Implications for March quarter GDP
We’re forecasting a 0.8% rise in GDP over the March quarter (released on 18 June). Today’s softer
than expected result follows firmer indications for the retail sector. We’ll take a closer look at how our
forecast for GDP growth is shaping up over the coming week as additional data on March quarter
activity is released.

Scenario analysis: How to position for Lagarde’s alternatives
The ECB is set to hike rates by 25bp on 11 June. Recent communication has effectively pre-
committed markets to this outcome. A hold would come as a shock and carry significant risk
for a central bank trying to avoid a repeat of the 2022 inflation crisis.
As such, our four scenarios for this meeting (above) exclude anything other than a 25bp move.

A 50bp hike could be included as an ultra-hawkish outcome, but there is already additional
tightening priced in for later this year, which means shocking markets now seems
unnecessary.
Focus on keeping markets hawkish
Markets are fully pricing in a 25bp hike, so focus will be on forward-looking language –
effectively a reality check on the 64bp of total tightening priced in by year-end. The Governing
Council and President Christine Lagarde will, in our view, share a key objective: to preserve

these hawkish market expectations.
That’s because the June move looks more like insurance than conviction. The risk of second-
round inflation effects remains as hard to estimate as the next chapter for the Gulf conflict and
energy prices. Inflation forecasts, typically central to the ECB reaction function, are now
heavily driven by oil prices and therefore less reliable in this environment. We think the real
battleground for the ECB now is inflation expectations. They are a function of rate

expectations, and unless the ECB keeps those hawkish, it risks de-anchoring.
So our baseline is a hawkish hike at this June meeting. Not so much because the ECB is
necessarily planning or willing to commit to another hike in July, September, or the fourth
quarter, but because it is the most prudent play as it waits for clarity on geopolitics,
energy, and the data. Our macro team has acknowledged rising risk of a follow-up move in
September, but our baseline remains that this June hike is a one-and-done.

Rates: ECB should tread carefully to keep inflation
expectations anchored
Markets are already fully pricing in this hike, but we think a confirmation could still add more
upward pressure on the curve. When paired with forward guidance about future hikes, we see
rates nudge slightly higher. More emphasis on inflation risks could increase markets’ pricing of
inflation too, adding to the upward pressure.

A (too) dovish outcome risks feeding future inflation expectations, paradoxically adding
upward pressure on rates further out the curve. This would entail a significant steepening with
2-year rates edging lower on less tightening, but a sticky 10-year rate on the back of inflation
concerns further out the future. A ‘one-and-done’ narrative would strongly push against the
positioning of current markets, which have already priced in a second hike by September. The

ECB should therefore likely prefer to come across as too hawkish rather than too dovish.
Markets are also sensitive to the growth outlook and have been moving on ECB comments
regarding the risks. So far, market sentiment has been very positive, helped by strong US
equity performance. But the balance is fragile and an ECB voicing more worries about the
growth outlook could pose some downward pressure across the entire curve.

About the Westpac card data indicators
The indicators presented in this report are based on the millions of credit and debit card
transactions processed every day. Transactions covering over ten million merchants are
classified into over 700 categories. These are in turn grouped into higher level aggregates

that provide a timely guide to wider economic trends.
The main focus of these indicators is consumer spending. Where possible, we have sought
to exclude ‘non spending’ transactions such as: money transfers; tax payments; loan
repayments; charitable donations; and superannuation contributions.
It should also be noted that these indicators will also be affected by shifts between card and
non card transactions. This was a significant factor during the COVID–19 pandemic – health

concerns about the use of physical cash leading to significantly higher use of cards vs cash,
particularly where contact–less transactions are available. Transaction flows also include
reversals/refunds which were also a significant phenomenon during the onset of COVID,
especially in areas such as travel.
All transaction data is compiled at a highly aggregated level so that individual customer or
merchant data is never revealed.
Index construction

The key metrics used in this report are indexes of spending–related card activity where the
base of 100 is average activity in 2019. As an example, if transaction flows are 5% above their
average level in 2019, the index read for the period is 105. If flows in a subsequent period are
8% above the average level in 2019, the index read for this period is 108. Growth between the
two periods can be calculated simply as the change between the two index reads, i.e. 2.9%.

All measures are adjusted for regular seasonality. Weekly estimates are generated using
the US Bureau of Labor’s MoveReg weekly seasonal adjustment program. Note that in some
cases, high levels of volatility during the COVID mean it is not possible to produce seasonally
adjusted estimates for some historical periods.
Also, note that previous versions of this report used different approaches to seasonal
adjustment and measurement more generally. This means Index reads are sometimes not

directly comparable. See the ‘About the Westpac card data indicators’ sections from earlier
reports to more detail.
Classifications
Note that the measures used for card data and in this report do not align completely with
the those used in official ABS statistics, including the ABS household spending indicator,
ABS retail trade survey and ABS estimate of consumer spending published in the national
accounts. There are a range of differences including around both coverage and classification.

As such, the card data should be treated as broadly indicative.
The transaction data is grouped into 26 categories that are then combined into four main as
follows:
Discretionary goods: alcoholic beverages, tobacco, clothing & footwear, furnishings &
household equipment, household appliances, vehicle–related, recreational & cultural goods,
newspaper, books & stationery, and other personal effects.
Discretionary services: transport services (part), recreational & cultural services, gambling,

catering services, accommodation services, other personal care, insurance & financial
services, other services.
Essential goods: food & non alcoholic beverages, medical products, appliances & equipment,
and operation of personal transport equipment.
Essential services: electricity, gas & other fuels, health services, transport services (part),
communications and education.
The report also uses two additional classifications:
MHSI/Retail: based on the extent to which categories cover activity that is in scope for the

ABS monthly household spending indicator and ABS retail trade survey.

非正規雇用の拡大、金融緩和の長期継続、バランスシートの改善が背景か 
水野・高橋（2002）に倣って、非金融企業の付加価値の分配状況を比較しても、対売上高比
と同様の傾向が確認される（図表 3）2。日本企業の当期純利益は、2000 年度の対付加価値比
3.0％から 2024 年度の同 25.1％へと、約 22.1％pt も上昇した。これは、米国企業の約 10.1％
pt の上昇（2000 年の同6.7％から2024 年の同16.8％）を大きく上回る。 
この背景には、労働分配率（人件費／付加価値）の低下3に加え、支払利息や特別損失の縮小
がある。 
法人企業統計における日本企業の労働分配率は、2000 年度の対付加価値比 73.2％から 2024
年度の同 64.2％へと、約 9.0％pt も低下した。同期間の米国（低下幅約 8.2％pt）とドイツ
（同 0.8％pt）と比較しても、日本の低下幅は相対的に大きい。これには、産業用ロボット導入
や第 4 次産業革命に伴う ICT の深化、非正規雇用の拡大、内部留保の積み上げといった要因が
指摘されている4。中でも、非正規雇用の拡大は日本特有の要因とみられる5。 
支払利息の低下は、日本銀行の超金融緩和政策と整合的な結果である。バブル崩壊後のデフ

月）といった非伝統的金融政策を導入した。リーマン・ショックをきっかけに、米国・欧州で
もこうした金融緩和政策が採用されたが、日本は他国に比べて長期間にわたり金融緩和を継続
した点が特徴的である。

同時に、日本企業は内部留保を積み上げてきた。内部留保は対付加価値比で 1.0％（2000 年

ずれも上昇ペースは緩やかであり、日本企業とは対照的である。 
日本企業が内部留保を積み上げた背景には、バブル崩壊後の長期的な低成長環境があると考
えられる。中小企業では、将来の資金繰りに対する備えとして予備的動機が働いた。一方、大
企業は、国内において潜在的な投資機会に直面していたものの、成長に対する不確実性などを
背景に積極的な設備投資に踏み切らなかった。その結果、内部留保が蓄積されたとみられる8。 
ただし、こうした内部留保の積み上げは近年、批判の対象となっている。内部留保が国内設
備投資に十分活用されず、主に投資有価証券や現預金として保有されている傾向が強いためだ。
特に、対外直接投資などの結果、投資有価証券の保有が増加した可能性が指摘されている9。

国内の設備投資に資金を回せるか 
過去 25 年間を振り返ると、日本企業が収益性を高めてきた要因としては、人件費、支払利
息、特別損失の減少が挙げられる。この傾向は、2000 年以降の日本企業の特徴として評価でき

こうした観点から課題となるのが、他国と比較しても高い水準にある内部留保である。日本

ないことが指摘されている。今後の成長のためには、これらの内部留保を国内の設備投資に積

２．中東情勢悪化の影響で先行きは下振れリスクが高い 
製造工業生産予測指数は、26 年 5 月が前月比
5.1％、6 月が同▲0.4％となった。生産計画の修
正状況を示す実現率（4 月） 、予測修正率（5 月）
はそれぞれ▲2.6％、0.2％であった。 
予測指数を業種別にみると、5 月は生産用機械
（前月比 16.0％） 、石油製品（同13.6％） 、輸送
機械（同 7.5％）が高い伸びとなっている。ただ
し、 石油製品の実現率は3 月 （▲9.4％） 、4 月 （▲
10.2％）と大幅なマイナスが続いており、実際の
生産は大きく下振れる可能性が高い。

26 年 4 月の生産指数を 5、6 月の予測指数で先延ばしすると、4-6 月期は前期比 3.1％となるが、
もともと実際の生産の伸びが計画を下回る傾向があることに加え、 中東情勢の影響で通常よりも下
振れ幅が大きくなる可能性がある。 
現時点では、 中東情勢悪化を受けた素材業種への下押し圧力を、グローバルなAI 関連需要を背景
とした加工業種の堅調が打ち消す形となっている。 しかし、中東情勢の混迷が長期化した場合には、
素材産業の悪化が加工業種へ波及し、生産全体が停滞するリスクが高まるだろう。
